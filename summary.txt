World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

US-brokered talks between Russia and Ukraine entered a second day in Abu Dhabi after nearly four hours Tuesday, the first such meeting in nine months. Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it constructive (BBC, PBS NewsHour).

Donald Trump said the Iran war was "effectively over" after meeting Qatar's premier, amid the US blockade. Secretary Rubio said there was no need to strike Iran again (Arab News, Reuters). An ADNOC vessel was damaged and three tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz (UKMTO); Brent rose to $92 (Reuters).

US prosecutors charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot against RAF Fairford in Britain (Justice Department).

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged balanced US-China trade — "de-risk, not decouple" — before a China trip; now on a military base after Iranian threats (South China Morning Post, The New York Times).

Anthropic filed to list on Nasdaq as ANT, seeking $64 billion, with Nvidia investing $10 billion (The New York Times). TSMC reported insatiable chip demand while planning stakes in Anthropic and OpenAI (Nikkei Asia).

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above forecasts (US Labor Department).

At COP31 in Antalya, a UN report found only 64 of 136 countries had early-warning capacity on all four measures (Reuters).

Israel's new Knesset was sworn in; President Herzog gave opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot 28 days to form a government (BBC). El Salvador's Bukele said his troops destroyed Haitian gang 400 Mawozo's headquarters; 20 reported dead, unconfirmed (BBC). In Indonesia, 120 MPs from eight factions moved to open an inquiry into Vice-President Gibran, a day after he told Prabowo he intends to resign over his voided school certificate (detikcom). The US House voted 211-204 to send Hegseth's impeachment to committee, effectively killing it; Rep. Thomas Massie was the sole Republican dissenter (NPR). A bus in Seongnam hit 32 vehicles and injured 57 people, nine seriously (Yonhap).
